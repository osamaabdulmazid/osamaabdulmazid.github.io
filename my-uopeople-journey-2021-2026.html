My UoPeople Journey: 2021–2026

The degree I had to keep choosing, even when life made it difficult.

By Osama Abdul Mazid University of the People Alumnus 2026

There are some achievements that look simple when you see them written on a résumé.

Bachelor of Science in Business Administration — 2026.

One line. One degree. One date.

But behind that line are five years of decisions, uncertainty, sacrifices, and countless moments when continuing would have been easier to postpone.

For me, earning this degree was never simply about getting a certificate.

It was about refusing to let difficult circumstances decide how far I could go.

It started in 2021

In September 2021, I started my journey with the University of the People.

At the time, I did not know exactly where this journey would take me. I only knew that I wanted to continue my education and build a future for myself.

The idea of studying online gave me an opportunity that felt possible when other educational paths did not.

But “possible” did not mean “easy.”

I did not have someone standing behind me financially, taking care of every educational expense, or making the journey comfortable. There were times when education itself became another problem to solve.

Sometimes the question was not, “What do I want to study?”

It was simply:

“How am I going to keep going?”

And somehow, I kept finding an answer.

I learned that continuing is also an achievement

People often celebrate the final result. They see the graduation. They see the degree. They see the smile in the photograph.

They rarely see the ordinary days that came before it.

The days when you have responsibilities outside university. The days when you are tired but still have coursework to complete. The days when financial pressure makes you question whether you should continue. The days when nobody is there to tell you, “Keep going. You are almost there.”

I had to learn to tell myself that.

Again and again.

I worked while continuing my education. I had professional responsibilities, deadlines, people to deal with, and a life outside the classroom.

There was no perfect schedule. There was no perfect situation.

I simply learned how to move forward with what I had.

And that changed the way I looked at education.

I stopped seeing a degree as something you earn only when everything in life is going well.

Sometimes, you earn it while life is happening around you.

More than a student

My relationship with the University of the People eventually became more than completing courses.

I became a UoPeople Ambassador.

That experience gave me something I did not expect when I first enrolled: the opportunity to help other people who were standing at the same starting line where I once stood.

I spoke with prospective students, answered questions, shared information, and tried to make the path a little clearer for people who were considering online education.

And there was something meaningful about that.

Because I understood the uncertainty.

I knew what it felt like to wonder whether an educational opportunity was actually possible for you.

So when someone asked questions about UoPeople, I wasn't simply speaking as an ambassador.

I was speaking as someone who had already walked part of the road.

Five years can change a person

When I look back at the person who started in 2021, I don't only see a student.

I see someone who had to become more disciplined. More patient. More independent. More comfortable with uncertainty.

University taught me business administration, but the journey taught me things that were harder to find in a textbook.

It taught me that progress doesn't always look impressive while you're living through it.

Sometimes progress is simply registering for another term.

Submitting another assignment.

Sitting for another exam.

Paying another educational expense.

Showing up again when you are already tired.

And then doing it again.

And again.

Until one day, you realize that the small decisions you kept making have taken you somewhere you once only hoped to reach.

2026

In 2026, I finally completed my undergraduate academic journey.

Bachelor of Science in Business Administration.

University of the People.

2021 → 2026.

When I reached the end, I didn't think only about the degree.

I thought about everything that had to happen for me to get there.

The uncertainty. The responsibilities. The financial pressure. The times I had to figure things out on my own. The people I met. The students I helped. The lessons I learned.

And all those ordinary days that never looked important at the time.

Suddenly, they all meant something.

The degree was the visible result. The journey was the real achievement.

If you're thinking about starting

If you're reading this because you are considering the University of the People, I don't want to tell you that the journey will be easy.

That would not be honest.

There will be deadlines. There will be difficult courses. There will be days when motivation disappears. And sometimes, life outside university will demand your attention more than your studies do.

But I would tell you something else:

Don't wait for your life to become perfect before you start building your future.

I didn't have a perfect situation. I didn't have every answer. I didn't have someone solving every problem for me.

I started with what I had.

And then I kept going.

If an online university can give someone a path to continue learning when circumstances make traditional education difficult, that opportunity can mean much more than a degree.

It can give you momentum. It can give you confidence. It can give you a reason to keep moving.

And sometimes, that is where change begins.

From student to alumnus

Today, I can finally say:

I am a University of the People alumnus.

But I don't want this to be the end of my story.

I want the degree to be a foundation for whatever comes next—professionally, academically, and personally.

I also hope that my journey can be useful to someone who is currently standing where I once stood.

Maybe you are worried about money. Maybe you are unsure whether you are capable. Maybe you have already stopped and started your education more than once. Maybe you feel like everyone else is moving forward while you are still trying to find your way.

If that is you, I have one thing to say:

You don't have to have the whole road figured out. You just have to decide whether you are willing to take the next step.

I took mine in September 2021.

Five years later, I reached the other side.
